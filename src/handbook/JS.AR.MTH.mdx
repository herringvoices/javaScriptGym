# Array Methods → The Boarblemish Sorting Terminal

Boarblemish Academy has a **sorting terminal** in the front hall. It takes raw lists of students, teachers, fines, and grades and prints the rosters that staff actually care about: who belongs in which house, who has pets, who owes fines, and who needs tutoring.

In this standard, you’ll turn those **plain arrays of objects** into meaningful output using **array methods**:

- `map` to transform each item
- `filter` to create focused subsets
- `find`, `some`, and `every` to answer questions about your lists
- `reduce` to summarize data into a single result

By the end, you’ll generate house rosters like this:

```txt
======DRAGONPORT ROSTER======
---------------------------
Edgar Gullylog
* Prof. Honeypot's Homeroom
* has fines
* needs tutoring
---------------------------
Polly Frumpleton
* Prof. Jellyroll's Homeroom
* needs tutoring
---------------------------
Pets in Dragonport: 6
---------------------------
```

All of this comes from a handful of **array methods** that accept **callback functions**. You’ll learn to write those callbacks, plug them into the right methods, and chain the steps together into readable “data pipelines.”

## 0) What you’re building

In this chapter, you’ll build a single file that gradually grows into the Boarblemish Sorting Terminal. The starter code gives you arrays like `students`, `teachers`, `fines`, and `grades`; your job is to turn them into useful views.

You’ll practice:

- Turning an array of student records into an **attendance list** (`"First Last"` names joined by commas).
- Sorting students into **houses** based on their `nature` field.
- Building lookup helpers that answer questions like “who is this student’s teacher?”, “does this student owe fines?”, and “do they need tutoring?”.
- Counting **pets per house** to produce little house summaries.
- Composing everything into formatted **house roster printouts** for all four Boarblemish houses.

Each section in the sidebar focuses on one tool (callbacks, `map`, `filter`, lookups, `reduce`, and final roster printing). Work through them in order—the later sections assume you’ve written the helpers from the earlier ones.
